# 1. Scene
One wide illustration set in Korea, divided exactly down the middle by a single vertical sepia ink line into two equal panels. The left panel is painted in faded sepia monochrome and the right panel in full warm colour. THEN: a 1970s Korean man in a worn suit standing before a closed heavy wooden office door in a dim Korean government corridor, holding a blank petition paper, a single clerk at a side window turning away. TODAY: a bright Korean National Assembly hearing room with a long curved wooden desk of Korean lawmakers in dark suits questioning a Korean official standing at a plain wooden podium, a television camera on a tripod at the side, Korean citizens watching from a gallery, tall windows and a plain wood-panelled wall with a blank nameboard behind the desk, the desk flanked by two bare wooden flagpoles standing empty. Each panel is a complete small scene with its own ground and sky, nothing crossing the centre line. Hand-painted watercolor and gouache illustration with fine sepia ink line work, visible cold-press paper grain, soft bleeding pigment edges, matte finish, the handwriting of an illustrated memoir picture book. Every newspaper page, calendar sheet, poster, banner, sign and screen is a plain blank surface. All faces are invented fictional Korean faces, everyone is unharmed and unarmed, and every crowd is peaceful.
